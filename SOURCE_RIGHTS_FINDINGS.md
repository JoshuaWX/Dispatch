# Dispatch source-rights findings (24 September 2026)

**Decision: keep autonomous publishing paused.** A conservative, potentially zero-fee route is to discover stories from first-party U.S. federal feeds and retrieve only agency-authored, unmarked public-domain text. This is a *candidate source policy*, not proof that the current pipeline can meet its four-article/three-domain, freshness, and corroboration gates on any given topic. A news-search API key does not license the linked publishers' article text. [17 U.S.C. § 105](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html); [USGS copyright FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-usgs-reportspublications-copyrighted); [TheNewsAPI terms](https://www.thenewsapi.com/tos).

## Rights that are relatively clear

USGS explains that this means works made by federal employees in their official duties, but that third-party works and foreign-country copyright can differ. | NOAA | NOAA Fisheries says most of its web text is not protected by U.S. copyright and marks copyrighted text; it asks for acknowledgement. The National Ocean Service says its content is public domain unless noted and cautions against implying endorsement or presenting modifications as official. [NOAA Fisheries policy](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/website-policies-and-disclaimers); [NOS FAQ](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/about/faq.html). | **Explicit, qualified reuse position**; page-by-page exclusions still required. |
| NWS / weather.gov | NWS expressly says web-page information is public domain unless noted and may be used without charge for any lawful purpose, subject to attribution/endorsement/misrepresentation conditions. [NWS disclaimer](https://www.weather.gov/disclaimer). | **Explicit, qualified permission**. NWS is part of NOAA, so its separate domain must not be treated as an independent *publisher* corroborating NOAA. |
| NASA | NASA says its media generally lacks U.S. copyright, permits factual/informational use, and has a specific AI-applications section. That section says AI products should be marked, the model's outputs attributed to the AI product rather than NASA, and NASA must not appear to verify or endorse generated claims. NASA also flags third-party copyrighted items and protected logos. [NASA media and AI guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). | **Qualified permission / caution.** The page focuses on media and public information, not an express blanket license for automated retrieval of every NASA article. Review authorship/third-party notices per page; keep an AI byline and source links without implying NASA approved the generated article. Seek NASA clarification before relying on ambiguous attribution language at scale. |

NASA, NOAA and USGS offer first-party discovery surfaces, including [NASA RSS feeds](https://www.nasa.gov/rss-feeds/), [NOAA National Ocean Service RSS feeds](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/rss.html), and [USGS news and feed links](https://www.usgs.gov/news). Using them is a **technical inference** for reducing dependence on commercial aggregators; the existence of a feed is not by itself an AI-use license. Apply rate limits, website notices, page-level rights checks, and the existing secure-fetch constraints. ## News-search API limits and uncertainty

- **NewsData.io (not the different `newsdata.ai` site):** first-party NewsData material conflicts about commercial use of its free tier: its [commercial-use comparison, updated 24 September 2026](https://newsdata.io/blog/free-news-api-for-commercial-use/) says **no** in a table, while its [free-API comparison](https://newsdata.io/blog/best-free-news-api/) says free-tier commercial use is allowed. Its [official dataset repository](https://github.com/newsdataapi/newsdata.io-free-datasets) separately warns that underlying publisher articles retain publisher copyrights even when the dataset compilation is CC BY. I could not verify a controlling NewsData.io API contract granting automated publisher-page retrieval or AI-generated derivative publication. **Treat both free-tier production rights and content-use rights as unresolved; do not enable it for this workflow without written clarification.**
- **TheNewsAPI:** its [published terms](https://www.thenewsapi.com/tos) grant only personal, non-commercial use of site content and say users must not access the “Site” by bots/scripts; they also state third-party websites and content have their own policies. Its [pricing page](https://www.thenewsapi.com/pricing) advertises API plans, including a free plan, but does not expressly grant downstream rights to fetch publisher pages or generate/re-publish AI articles. Whether the site's bot clause covers intended authenticated API calls is **ambiguous**. **Do not treat a search result or paid plan as publisher-content permission; obtain API-specific written clarification before production reliance.**
- **The Guardian:** its [Open Platform terms](https://www.theguardian.com/open-platform/terms-and-conditions) expressly prohibit AI-related extraction and use to generate/synthesise content, and prohibit automated collection, including from the Guardian Digital Network. **Exclude Guardian content absent a separate negotiated license.**

## Feasibility against Dispatch's publication gate

Four accessible, dated *illustrations* from three agency-controlled registrable domains are [NASA: 2026 Arctic sea-ice minimum (23 Sep)](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/climate-science/sea-ice/arctic-sea-ice-2026-min/), [NASA: Arctic melt-season study (21 Sep)](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/arctic-melt-season-length-levels-off/), [NOAA Fisheries: Aquaculture Week (21 Sep)](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/celebrating-aquaculture-week-farming-tide-table), and [USGS: inland-fisheries visualizations (22 Sep)](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/climate-adaptation-science-centers/news/new-data-visualizations-dive-inland-fisheries-food). **These do not form one qualifying evidence package:** they are unrelated subjects, and the two NASA pages are not independent corroboration. This search has **not** demonstrated four same-topic pages across three independent publishers, with two pages in 72 hours and a corroborated material claim. Page dates and author/rights status must be verified afresh at run time. [NASA article 1](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/climate-science/sea-ice/arctic-sea-ice-2026-min/); [NASA article 2](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/arctic-melt-season-length-levels-off/); [NOAA article](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/celebrating-aquaculture-week-farming-tide-table); [USGS article](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/climate-adaptation-science-centers/news/new-data-visualizations-dive-inland-fisheries-food).

**Recommended next gate:** implement first-party-feed discovery and per-page rights/provenance checks in staging, using only reviewed agency text and no arbitrary publisher fetches. Record original author, publication date, license/public-domain notice, excluded third-party blocks, and source URL. A bare domain allowlist is insufficient because each agency warns of third-party content. Run an evidence-only sample over several days; enable the Gemini soak only if an actual same-topic four-URL/three-independent-publisher set passes the existing gates, or obtain explicit publisher/API licenses. A sparse/no-publication result is safer than weakening the evidence gate. [USGS FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-usgs-reportspublications-copyrighted); [NOAA policy](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/website-policies-and-disclaimers); [NASA guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).
