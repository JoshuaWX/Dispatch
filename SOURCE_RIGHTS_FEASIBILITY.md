# Source-rights feasibility for Dispatch (24 September 2026)

**Finding: a no-fee source-discovery path is plausible, but autonomous publication is not yet launch-ready.** First-party terms support commercial adaptation of some government and Creative Commons text, and several publishers provide machine-readable discovery. I did **not** verify one same-topic package with four article-level URLs from three independent publishing organisations/domains, two articles *published* within the preceding 72 hours, and an independently corroborated material claim. Do not lower that gate or start the publishing soak on the strength of these rights findings alone.

| Source | Reuse / AI-summary right | Automated retrieval | Dispatch decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GOV.UK** (`gov.uk`) | The [Open Government Licence v3](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/) expressly allows copying, adapting, publishing and commercial use of covered information, with attribution, no endorsement, and exclusions for third-party rights. The [GOV.UK reuse page](https://www.gov.uk/help/reuse-govuk-content) applies OGL to its content except where stated. AI summarisation is **an inference from the broad adaptation grant**, not an AI-specific statement. | **Explicit:** GOV.UK permits its [Content API](https://content-api.publishing.service.gov.uk/) for anyone and any purpose, without authentication, at no more than 10 requests/second. It also [permits scraping](https://www.gov.uk/help/reuse-govuk-content) subject to robots.txt and rate limits. | Best first implementation target; retain each page's rights notice and organisation metadata. Multiple GOV.UK agencies still count as **one registrable domain** under the current three-domain rule. |
| **ECDC** (`europa.eu`) | ECDC-owned web text is [CC BY 4.0](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ecdc-intellectual-property-notices): commercial adaptation is expressly allowed with credit, licence link and change indication; third-party material is excluded. [Creative Commons' FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) explains that CC permissions cover new technologies where copyright permission is required. This supports AI-assisted derivative summaries **as an inference**, not an ECDC-specific AI approval. | ECDC offers [first-party RSS feeds](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/rss-feeds) for news and reports. The feed is an authorized discovery surface; I did not find express permission for unrestricted full-page crawling. | Candidate with feed discovery and conservative, robots-aware page fetch; verify ECDC ownership/third-party blocks per page. All ECDC/EU Commission/Eurostat subdomains of `europa.eu` count as **one registrable domain**, and agency independence cannot be inferred from hostname. |
| **European Commission / Eurostat** (`europa.eu`) | Commission-owned site content is [CC BY 4.0](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en), with third-party/identity/trademark exclusions. [Eurostat expressly licenses its owned editorial text CC BY 4.0](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice). | Eurostat describes [RSS as automated news distribution](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/rss). This authorizes feed consumption, not arbitrary full-site crawling. | Useful additional material, but **not** a second or third domain alongside ECDC. Some claims may originate in shared EU data; check independent corroboration. |
| **Global Voices** (`globalvoices.org`) | Its [republishing guidelines](https://globalvoices.org/about/global-voices-attribution-policy/) say Global-Voices-created stories are CC BY and can be adapted for any purpose, even commercially; author, original link, licence, and changes need clear attribution. Third-party photos/video/audio are excluded. Its [AI policy](https://globalvoices.org/about/global-voices-policy-on-ai/) rejects automatically generated *submissions to its own newsroom*; I found no express revocation of downstream CC BY rights, but using its reporting for automated articles merits direct clarification and respectful attribution. | Its [RSS page](https://globalvoices.org/feeds/) welcomes feed embedding/aggregation, including story feeds. That is explicit automated **feed** distribution; full-page retrieval beyond feeds was not expressly addressed. | Potential independent news publisher, subject to page-level ownership, feed/robots limits and the AI-policy clarification. Its translated versions and subdomains are **not** independent publishers. |
| **U.S. federal agency-authored text** (`nasa.gov`, `noaa.gov`, `usgs.gov`, `cdc.gov`) | [17 U.S.C. §105](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html) excludes U.S.-government works from U.S. copyright; [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-usgs-reportspublications-copyrighted) and [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html) warn that contractor, third-party, and foreign-jurisdiction rights differ. CDC additionally requires source attribution, a non-endorsement disclaimer, and no substantive alteration when using its public-domain content. AI-generated analysis should not be presented as a modified official CDC statement. | Some agencies offer feeds and APIs, but a feed is not a whole-site crawling grant. Follow each publisher's robots and site terms. | Candidate only for demonstrably agency-authored, unmarked text; review individual articles. Distinct federal agencies can be independent publishers, while two NOAA subdomains cannot. NASA and NSIDC sometimes jointly announce the **same** measurement, so their reports do not automatically corroborate independently. |

**Do not treat these as blanket substitutes:** [Met Office terms](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/policies/tandc) apply OGL to much text but explicitly prohibit robots/page-scraping except authorised APIs; its RSS terms permit feed use but restrict editing feed content. [WHO copyright policy](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright) commonly licenses publications CC BY-NC-SA and requires permission for commercial uses. [ESA publications](https://www.esa.int/About_Us/ESA_Publications/Copyright) do not grant derivative-work rights. Canada's [open-government licence](https://open.canada.ca/en/open-government-licence-canada) is broad for covered information, but [Canada.ca's general terms](https://www.canada.ca/en/transparency/terms.html) separately require written permission for commercial redistribution of site material unless otherwise specified; a whole-domain allowlist would be unsafe. [Wikinews became read-only in May 2026](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation_Board_noticeboard/Archives/2026), so its open archive cannot meet a current-news freshness gate.

## Contemporary sample tested: DRC Ebola outbreak

These four first-party pages cover the same ongoing outbreak across three organisations/domains: [ECDC deployment, 21 September](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/news-events/ecdc-deploys-experts-drc-increase-support-amidst-ongoing-ebola-outbreak); [UK government funding, 1 September](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-accelerates-support-for-efforts-to-stop-ebola-outbreak-at-source); [UKHSA outbreak monitoring, updated 17 September](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/outbreaks-under-monitoring-in-2026/outbreaks-under-monitoring-week-36-week-ending-6-september-2026); and [CDC MMWR, 15 September](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7537e1.htm). **This is not a qualifying publication pack:** only the ECDC article was newly published close to 24 September; the UKHSA page's update cannot be counted as a newly published article; the two UK pages share `gov.uk`; CDC's MMWR needs author/rights checking rather than assuming every CDC-hosted paper is a U.S.-government work. A [21 September UK statement about Burundi](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/un-human-rights-council-63-uk-statement-for-the-interactive-dialogue-on-burundi) mentions Ebola only tangentially, so it is not a legitimate second fresh Ebola article. [Africa CDC did publish an Ebola release on 20 September](https://africacdc.org/news-item/africa-cdc-welcomes-u-s-convened-g20-ebola-meeting-and-calls-for-united-action-faster-delivery-and-full-transparency/), but I found no first-party licence granting Dispatch automated AI-derivative use of its article text; do not count it as rights-cleared.

**Implementation/release gate:** build an evidence-only staging sampler from the first-party feeds/API above, store page-level licence/author/ownership/robot checks, group by actual publisher organisation as well as registrable domain, and record *original publication time* separately from updates. Require a real qualifying package, then run Gemini staging and the 48-hour soak. This is a plausible path to **sparse** autonomous publication, not evidence that a two-hour schedule will yield an article every run. If no package materialises, obtain explicit publisher/API permissions; keep publishing paused rather than treating snippets, refreshed pages, or affiliated sources as independent evidence.
