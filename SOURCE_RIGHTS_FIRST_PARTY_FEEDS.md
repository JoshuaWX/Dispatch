# First-party feed and text-reuse findings (24 September 2026)

This note supports the **new** Dispatch policy: a narrowly described, verified announcement may rely on its **issuing authoritative organisation's own** eligible page; a developing or disputed story needs genuinely independent organisations and original evidence. Feed entries are discovery leads, never evidence by themselves. Inspect the final article-level page, its author and rights notices, and its original publication timestamp before drafting. The earlier four-URL/three-domain research notes describe the *superseded* policy, not a reason to block this new route.

Do not treat a NASA-USGS joint announcement as two independent sources. |
| **NASA** | Official [NASA RSS directory](https://www.nasa.gov/rss-feeds/) offers news-release RSS at `https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/feed/`; [robots.txt](https://www.nasa.gov/robots.txt) allows general crawling. | NASA's [media/AI rules](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) permit factual source disclosure but require AI outputs to be attributed to the AI product, prohibit phrases such as “according to NASA” in AI products, and prohibit implied NASA review/endorsement; third-party NASA-hosted media do not inherit NASA's permissions. ## Independence, timestamps, and provenance rules for implementation

1. Persist `canonical_url`, feed URL, issuing organisation, author/owner where available, licence URL and scope, attribution text, rights-check result/time, original publication time, update time, retrieval time, excerpt/hash and any declared upstream release, dataset, study or joint-press reference. Source IDs must resolve to these fetched pages, not feed snippets or homepages. The licence is a property of the **article/excerpt**, not just its host.
2. For an official-announcement brief, one eligible page is enough only for propositions the issuing authority can directly attest (e.g., “the agency announced…”, “the agency published…”, the stated scope/date of its own decision). Unverified efficacy, impact, third-party allegations, scientific certainty or a rapidly evolving incident need independent corroboration or a narrower claim. Keep the source link, accurate uncertainty and AI-authorship notice.
3. For developing stories, count independent **originating organisations and observations**, not URL/domain quantity. Two pages from one agency, translations, wire copies, joint statements, or NASA/NSIDC pages repeating a shared measurement are **one origin**. GOV.UK department and ECDC reports about different data they independently collected could be two only if the specific corroborated claim is separately supported; a UK statement reciting ECDC/WHO numbers is not.
4. RSS/Atom `pubDate`/`updated` can be revised; take the article's original date where available (GOV.UK `first_published_at` is explicit), record subsequent edits separately, reject missing/ambiguous original dates for freshness-sensitive claims, and never relabel an old page as new based on feed recirculation.
5. At every two-hour run use conditional requests/caching, a descriptive user agent, low per-host concurrency, five-second page deadlines, robots checks and 429 `Retry-After` backoff. This is an engineering safety recommendation, **not** a claim that all five publishers have given a numeric crawl quota. The GOV.UK reuse page warns that excessive requests are rate-limited; ECDC/USGS limits remain unverified. An inaccessible or rights-unclear page fails closed before Gemini.

### Evidence limits

The listed URLs and rights notices were checked from publishers' first-party pages on 24 September 2026. Read-only direct HTTP probes also parsed GOV.UK, ECDC, and USGS feeds on this workstation; see corrections below. These do **not** prove staging/production reachability, complete page extraction, a qualifying story, or a publisher endorsement of Dispatch's AI workflow.

### Direct-feed probe corrections (24 September 2026)

* GOV.UK Atom GET returned **200**. Entry fields: `id,updated,link,title,summary` (no `published`). It mixes `/government/publications/` with `/government/news/`, so require Content API `schema_name=news_article` and an allowed `document_type`. A sampled news item had `first_published_at`, `public_updated_at`, `details.body`, and `links.organisations`. The active page's `withdrawn_notice` was `{}`; test for substantive notice fields, not object truthiness.
* ECDC RSS GET returned **200**. Fields: `title,link,description,pubDate,creator,guid`. Its first item was a topic landing page, not an article. A sampled `/en/news-events/` page had canonical URL, `article:published_time`, a dated `<time>`, `<article class="ct-news ... full">`, and body under that article's `div.wysiwyg-content`. Scope to the full article; related cards also use `<article>`.
* USGS RSS and sampled article normal GET returned **200**; the browsing tool's earlier 405 was tool-specific. RSS fields: `title,link,description,pubDate,creator,guid,source`. Strip UTM from item URLs and use page canonical. Sample HTML had an internal “Communications and Publishing” byline, date in `div.owner-date span.date`, and body in `div.node-main-body` / `field--name--field-content`. No structured publication-time meta or page-specific licence link was seen. The feed head was dated 3 September; no guarantee of a fresh item every run.
* NIH RSS direct GET returned **403 Forbidden**. Despite its published feed link and broad public-domain notice, disable it until staging can fetch normally without evasion. No NIH feed schema is asserted here. NASA and Eurostat XML contents were not parsed.

The earlier USGS row's 405 warning is superseded by the direct GET result above; the earlier NIH row's general recommendation is superseded by the 403 fail-closed decision. OGL/CC BY adaptation coverage for Gemini-assisted summaries remains an inference from the licence grants, not a publisher-specific endorsement.
